About

I am the technical lead on Tesla's internal GenAI platform and manager of the product team on it. Over the past year the platform grew roughly 6× in GPU capacity across three datacenters and 55× in direct users, serving models company-wide across billions of requests.

I built the provider- and API-agnostic inference gateway covering chat, embeddings, and image and video generation, so Claude Code, Codex, OpenCode, Grok CLI and the rest work against one endpoint for every Tesla employee. I own the metering and access-governance layer end to end — versioned rate cards, per-org cost attribution, automated spend limits, manager-chain approval — which replaced a manual approval queue with a token service and took the platform out of the critical path for every new consumer.

I am also the sole author of the inference benchmarking harness the platform's capacity decisions run on. Its time-to-first-token data was the empirical case behind the proposals that grew the fleet and put frontier open-weight models in house.

I came to infrastructure through hardware. Three years in Tesla's Vision R&D division building computer vision and opto-electro-mechanical inspection systems: optical and photometric design, camera interfacing, embedded lighting and motion control in C, robotic-arm inspection cells, the detection and segmentation models on top, and the programs that rolled them out globally. My M.Sc. from the University of Washington specialised in controls and robotics, with a patent and a publication in adaptive lighting control. Before that, six years in Middle East oil and gas running cost, schedule, and risk on capital projects.

That path is why I reach for first principles and a number when a problem is unfamiliar. Throughout Tesla I go by “The Janitor”, earned cleaning up messes and making sure everything works properly and reliably while delivering critical solutions.
