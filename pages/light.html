Light Simulation

Where does the light land?

Place lamps and parts, and read the illuminance falling on every surface, in lux or in watts per square metre, from the same stored measurement. Radiance is carried as 95 spectral bins, so the photometric numbers come from an exact integral against the CIE observer rather than a correction factor.

Ask the assistant, or tell it what to do

Commands are executed exactly and need no model at all: try “load the inspection workcell”, “switch to W per square metre”, “turn off interreflection” or “what does it read”. Prose questions need a small language model, which downloads into this browser once and then answers on your own GPU, with nothing sent anywhere.

Why the unit switch is instant

Every internal quantity is radiometric. Lux exists only as an exact integral of the stored spectrum against the CIE luminous efficiency function V(λ), never as a fudge factor on a radiometric number.

Direct light from a source is always that source's normalised spectrum scaled by one number, so what is stored per point is that number, per lamp. Both lux and W·m⁻² are linear functionals of a spectrum, so each is a different dot product against the same stored row. Changing units, or isolating one lamp, is arithmetic over numbers already in memory, not another solve.

What it is doing

Direct light is next-event estimation against every lamp, with visibility, so shadows are real. Interreflection is a path traced with multiple importance sampling, accumulated progressively; watch the numbers settle after a change. A lamp given in lumens is converted to watts once, when the scene loads, so no photometric value is ever stored or accumulated.

This is a JavaScript port of Light-Simulation, my spectral ray tracer in C. It reproduces that engine's spectral and colorimetric output to all 18 significant digits, and its 4096-point workcell field to within float32 round-off, using the same random stream, so the comparison is of numbers rather than of averages. The scene format is the CLI's, so a scene you download here runs there unchanged. The workcell preset is the geometry behind US Patent 12,610,446 and the adaptive-lighting work in my thesis.
